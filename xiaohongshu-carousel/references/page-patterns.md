# Page Patterns

Use these patterns as starting points, not mandatory templates. A carousel works best when adjacent pages use visibly different compositions while retaining the same visual system.

## Recommended Sequence

1. **Cover:** Literal topic title, one image signal, minimal background.
2. **Why it matters:** A map, key fact, misconception, or frame that makes the rest worth reading.
3. **Core structure:** A timeline, steps, or a central model.
4. **Deep dive:** One landmark, concept, or case examined closely.
5. **Compare:** Parallel columns or panels using identical labels and scale.
6. **Change or cause:** Before/after, problem/response, or turning point.
7. **Lived experience:** Scene, people, routine, or practical consequence.
8. **Present-day link:** How the earlier material connects to current reality or a traveler/reader decision.
9. **Close:** A compact checklist, takeaway, or visual release. Omit it if the previous page already ends cleanly.

## Copy Density

- One page should answer one question. Split a page if it needs several competing headings.
- Use a title plus 2-4 short lines for most slides. Timelines and comparisons may use more, but keep their modules parallel.
- Place factual qualifiers where they prevent a likely misunderstanding, not as a wall of caveats.
- Use small-detail labels only when they add a memorable fact; merge them into the related concept card when separate placement becomes repetitive.

## Overlay Rules

- Put text in the low-detail portion of the image, or use a restrained opaque/translucent backing when the subject requires a busy background.
- Use connectors only to clarify a relationship. Start at the label and end precisely on the referenced landmark, object, or region.
- Keep numbers and counts as controlled vector text/icons. Counted decorative marks are useful only when they agree with the stated fact.
- Leave sufficient card padding for the longest anticipated Chinese line. Render at final resolution before accepting the layout.

## Map And Diagram Rules

- Prefer a real or deliberately schematic map over a generative pseudo-map when geography is part of the claim.
- State the relationship the reader should understand directly on the page: route, boundary, direction, distance, or strategic position.
- Do not place decorative water, coastlines, bridges, or landmarks where they imply a false location.

## Image Generation Prompt Checklist

Describe:

- intended page role and vertical ratio;
- scene and main subject;
- visual medium and relationship to the existing set;
- composition and exact clear area reserved for overlays;
- factual cues that need to be plausible;
- explicit exclusions: text, letters, logos, watermarks, unwanted landmarks, or a style the user rejected.

After generation, inspect the bitmap rather than accepting the prompt intent as evidence.
